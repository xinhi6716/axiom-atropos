# Read the Original Work

Use this guidance when literature must support a scientific judgment or the user asks to read a paper. Abstracts help find and prioritize papers; they do not substitute for the original evidence. Follow the [identity and access rules](evidence-reasoning.md#preserve-evidence-boundaries), [source safety](source-safety.md), and existing access authorization.

## Obtain readable original material

Start with a supplied file or a reliable article link already available. Otherwise seek the publisher's article body, a lawful repository copy or accepted manuscript, or a source-native full-text service. Use actual tool capabilities; no specific connector or PDF format is required. Match the work and version before using the content. A supplement, correction, review, or citing paper is a separate object, not a replacement for the target article.

Inspect the returned content. A successful download, a tool called "read paper", or a page labeled "full text" may yield only a landing page, abstract, machine summary, login screen, or truncated extraction. A model-generated paper summary is a navigation aid, not a direct reading of the paper. Check whether the body sections and relevant objects are actually present. A file on disk is available material; parsing or searching it does not mean the model has read it.

Prefer readable HTML/XML when it preserves the relevant structure; use PDF or rendered pages when layout, figures, equations, or extraction quality requires them. Follow links to the article body and supplements rather than repeatedly searching for copies of the same abstract. If one representation is incomplete, use an available lawful alternative or read the missing pages/sections; do not infer missing text from the title, abstract, or another model's summary. Install a reader or use an external processing service only within existing authorization.

## Choose reading depth

Use the next reading action to resolve a concrete uncertainty. These are choices, not a mandatory full-paper pass for every search hit.

| Current situation | Reading needed | What this permits |
|---|---|---|
| Clearly unrelated hit, metadata task, or initial discovery | Title/identity/abstract sufficient for that decision; open missing details only when fit is ambiguous. | Bibliographic facts, provisional screening or an attributed abstract summary; no checked scientific result. |
| Plausible candidate for consequential evidence, ambiguous eligibility, or unfamiliar/complex article | Inspect the original article's structure and study overview, then follow the relevant evidence route below. | A reading map and firmer eligibility; headings alone do not establish support or reliability. |
| A specific result, number, mechanism or recommendation will support the answer | Read the result with its necessary methods/assumptions, controls, definitions, uncertainty, limitations and cross-referenced objects. | Support for the checked claim within its actual conditions; no implication that all sections were read. |
| The user requests a whole-paper appraisal, or unresolved connections among sections could change the judgment | Read substantive sections and main objects across the article; expand to the relevant supplements and linked original findings. | An article-level appraisal when material unread sections are accounted for; do not claim reproduction or validate every inference by default. |

Escalate when a needed definition or control is elsewhere, a result has competing interpretations, a figure/supplement carries decisive evidence, or reports conflict. Stop expanding when the requested judgment is supported at its stated scope and the remaining material cannot change it. Existing verified reading can be reused; a narrower claim does not require rereading unrelated sections.

## Map the original before locating evidence

For a plausible key candidate or article appraisal, first inspect a lightweight map of the actual original: table of contents if present, otherwise section/subsection headings, figure/table list or captions, and supplement links. Check the study overview for what was tested and where methods, results, limitations and versions/corrections reside. Research articles often lack a formal contents page; use their structure instead of inventing a directory or refusing to proceed.

The map should reveal where the answer could be found, including alternative explanations and boundary results, rather than select only passages matching the desired conclusion. A short paper or an already mapped source may need only a brief scan. For long or multi-study papers, retain the relevant section/page/anchor ranges and which experiments each object belongs to. If supplied excerpts omit the structure, record that limit and read the available context without claiming an article-wide map.

A contents scan is orientation, not whole-paper reading. A section heading such as “mechanism” does not show that a mechanism was proved. Follow the [reliability appraisal](evidence-reasoning.md#appraise-reliability-separately-from-relevance) in the sections that can answer it.

## Locate evidence and test the pairing

Break the intended assertion into its material components, such as the tested object, intervention/relation, outcome, value, comparator, conditions and causal strength. Use the map and source search to find candidate passages with synonyms, method names, variables or cited table/figure identifiers; absence of one exact phrase is not absence of evidence.

Read the passage and enough neighboring context to establish what the statement refers to. Follow its method, table/figure, equation, supplement or original-study cross-reference when consequential. Distinguish this paper's own finding from its Introduction's account of earlier literature and from the authors' suggested explanation. A Discussion sentence that cites another study may require reading that original study rather than using this paper as the primary evidence.

Close the check with `proposed assertion → original observation and locator → conditions/design → supports, limits, contradicts or unresolved`. Include a short faithful observation or data row, not just a page number or “evidence found”. Verify every decisive component; one supported number does not close a mechanism, duration or transfer assertion in the same sentence. Preserve source wording, units, uncertainty and qualifications, and test whether neighboring text or another relevant object changes the interpretation. Apply [claim-specific support](evidence-reasoning.md#claim-specific-relevance-and-support) and [source comparison](evidence-reasoning.md#brief-source-comparison).

Use section/subsection plus a distinguishing paragraph phrase, PDF page with printed-page clarification where needed, stable HTML anchor, table row/column/footnote, figure panel/caption, or equation/assumption identifiers as appropriate. Do not invent paragraph/page numbers for unpaginated HTML or a generated extraction. When extraction makes a decisive element ambiguous, inspect an available original representation before relying on it. Keep a missing or unread object specifically pending; finding a convenient passage does not close it.

## Read for the requested scope

Distinguish two tasks:

- **Claim verification:** read the original result with enough surrounding methods, definitions, controls, assumptions, and limitations to assess that claim. Search terms locate passages; isolated search hits are not the reading itself.
- **Whole-paper reading or critical appraisal:** work through the article's substantive sections, main figures/tables and captions, and the relevant supplements. Cover the question and prior-work context, design or derivation, results, interpretation, and limitations. Do not call a targeted passage read or an abstract/conclusion pair a whole-paper reading.

For a long paper, make a small map of sections, page ranges, figures, and linked supplements. Read it in manageable, identifiable chunks and keep track of what was read, pending, inaccessible, or damaged. Use heading, page, paragraph, table/figure identifiers or stable anchors rather than inventing page numbers for HTML. Follow cross-references that matter to the interpretation. Read past tool-output limits; a truncated return is not the end of the article. Maintain this coverage in existing notes when needed, not a mandatory new file for every paper.

Adapt the route to the work: an experimental claim may depend on the protocol and controls; a theoretical result on definitions, assumptions, derivation, and proof; a computational result on data splits, baselines, metrics, and leakage. Do not force every discipline into an experimental template. A whole-paper task must account for unread substantive sections before claiming completion; unrelated references or ancillary files need not be read recursively.

## Connect the result to what produced it

For the finding that will support the answer, identify the object or population, study design, comparator, measurement or mathematical definition, conditions and time span, result and uncertainty, and the qualifications that could change its meaning. Locate these in the body rather than filling them from expected practice. Distinguish independent units from repeated observations and the tested intervention from background treatment or preparation.

Preserve the source's actual entities, measurement types, and relationships when taking notes and drafting. Do not supply an unstated hierarchy, setting, or more specific measured quantity from plausibility; leave it unspecified or check the original passage. Before delivery, compare the consequential paraphrase with that passage, including whether a claimed relation was actually reported.

Read the table/figure caption, axes, legend, footnotes, units, denominator, and matching methods when they affect a quantitative claim. Compare the relevant panel or row rather than the headline alone. When an equation matters, inspect its definitions and domain of validity; the displayed formula alone may omit essential assumptions.

For a claimed range, maximum, or universal result, check the relevant boundary entries under their actual conditions. Preserve distinctions such as "less than" versus "at most", and "all tested conditions" versus a supported subset. Reporting the correct table value does not by itself resolve a conflicting abstract statement; carry that comparison into the answer. Do not create a discrepancy from ordinary rounding or incomparable conditions.

When a design estimate supports a consequential performance, resource, or operating-duration claim, verify its decisive arithmetic, unit conversions, and assumptions. Compare the estimate with the associated claims in both the article and supplement, distinguishing configurations and nominal ratings from measured operation. Attribute an unverified calculation as the source's estimate; a corrected calculation is your derivation, not a new measurement.

Inspect the rendered page or figure when the conclusion depends on visual content, or when extraction may have lost signs, exponents, subscripts, columns, labels, or footnotes. Text extraction alone does not establish a morphology, spatial relationship, curve shape, or panel comparison. Label values estimated from a plot; do not fabricate precision or treat an OCR ambiguity as a scientific discrepancy. If visual inspection is unavailable, state which observation remains unverified and avoid the dependent claim, including claims that information is absent when it may appear in an unread figure.

Read a supplement when a required protocol, uncertainty, control, definition, or validation is placed there. A statement that methods are "in the supplement" is a pointer to read, not evidence that they were checked. If it remains unavailable, preserve that specific limit. Absence from a retrieved fragment does not establish absence from the paper.

Check relevant Discussion/limitations passages for scope, alternatives, and failed or conditional results. Explicitly compare the abstract's and conclusion's decision-relevant claims with the original results and their qualifications: direction/magnitude, uncertainty or significance, duration/population, and causal or mechanistic wording. An abstract's omission of detail is not itself a contradiction; check whether it actually asserts something the original evidence does not establish. An interval including the null is not proof of no effect, and a significance discrepancy requires comparing the same outcome, estimand, analysis, and uncertainty definition rather than assuming all intervals and tests are equivalent.

Capture the results of that comparison in the [brief source comparison](evidence-reasoning.md#brief-source-comparison), including compatible statements. When body, abstract, captions, or versions disagree, apply the [within-source conflict rule](evidence-reasoning.md#fact-checking). Retain both statements and their locators, the reconciliation attempted, and the effect on the conclusion. Do not automatically prefer the strongest statement or silently replace it with the weaker one.

## Use only what the reading supports

Before a paper supports a consequential conclusion, retain a concise connection among the claim, original source/version, actual passage or visual object read, locator, and the conditions/limitations relevant to that claim. A short faithful observation is sufficient; a verbatim quote is not mandatory and cannot replace interpreting a figure or equation. Keep source findings separate from the author's explanation and your own inference.

Abstract-only candidates can remain in the discovery list or field map as unverified leads, but do not count them as supporting studies, independent corroboration, or inputs to a quantitative comparison. Labeling a conclusion "abstract-level" does not make it a supported scientific conclusion. An explicitly requested abstract summary can faithfully describe what the abstract reports while stating that the underlying finding has not been checked.

When needed original material is unavailable, pursue feasible lawful access or an adequately verified alternative source within scope. Do not downgrade the evidence requirement silently, substitute agreement among abstracts, or repeat equivalent failed requests. Continue independent work and mark the affected claim unresolved. Seek user input only when access, scope, resources, or authorization actually requires it; use the [full-text checkpoint](interactive-workflow.md#full-text-checkpoint). Report the best supported answer and the concrete missing material, rather than implying that cautious phrasing completes an unmet original-source task.
